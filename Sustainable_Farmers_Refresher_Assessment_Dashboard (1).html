Summary & Assessment Overview

Participation, assessment coverage and the main adoption signals.

Summary

The assessment covered 42 farmers, of whom 37 attended the refresher training. Reported adoption was strongest for composting, raised beds, crop rotation/intercropping and African leafy vegetables (ALVs). Adoption of Azolla and vermicomposting remained limited. Farmers highlighted raised beds and composting as useful practices, while requests for materials, group support and demonstration opportunities point to the need for continued follow-up.

Percentages for practice adoption generally use the 37 training attendees as the denominator. Some questions allow multiple responses, so totals may exceed 100%.

Adoption priorities

  • Provide hands-on demonstrations for Azolla and vermicomposting, which most attendees had not started.
  • Strengthen compost management, especially moisture monitoring.
  • Support farmers to sustain raised beds, kitchen gardens and ALV production.
  • Promote integrated pest management, including crop rotation/intercropping, botanical pesticides and biological control.

Other findings

  • Crop variety was reported as the same by 21 farmers (57%); 13 (35%) reported much less.

  • Organic topdressers: 22 (59%) reported none.

Enumerator Observations

Qualitative themes recorded during assessment visits.

Positive progress

  • Visible progress in composting, ALV production and raised-bed establishment was noted.
  • Some farmers were diversifying crops and using organic topdressers.
  • Raised beds and composting were frequently identified as useful practices.

Gaps and barriers

  • Adoption and consistency of practice varied among farmers.
  • Some compost heaps had gaps in management, including moisture monitoring and regular turning.
  • Limited land, competing tasks and low commitment were reported as barriers in some cases.
  • Adoption of Azolla and vermicomposting remained low.

Recommended follow-up

  • Prioritise hands-on demonstrations for practices with low adoption.
  • Provide practical guidance on compost moisture, turning and readiness.
  • Mobilise farmer groups for peer support and shared learning.
  • Plan follow-up visits and document changes in adoption over time.

Data interpretation

Enumerator observations are qualitative themes and should not be interpreted as counts unless the original records have been coded and tallied. The assessment should be used to guide targeted support rather than assume every farmer faces the same barriers.
